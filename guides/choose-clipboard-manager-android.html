What to look for in a free clipboard manager for Android

Updated 2026-10-30

A clipboard manager keeps the things you copy so you can paste them again later. On Android they differ more than they look, mainly because Android limits how apps can read the clipboard. Here is what to check before you choose one.

1. How it saves what you copy

Since Android 10, only the app on screen (and the keyboard you are typing with) can read the clipboard. A clipboard app sitting in the background cannot see what you copy in other apps, whatever its store page suggests.

So check how an app actually saves: when you open it, from a floating button or widget, from the text-selection menu, or with an Accessibility-based auto-save that notices the moment you copy. An app that is honest about this saves you surprises later.

2. What it keeps

Some apps keep only text. If you copy screenshots, photos or images from the web, look for one that saves images too, with a preview you can open.

3. How quickly you get things back

Search matters once the history grows. Pinning and groups (snippets) keep addresses, account numbers and canned replies at hand. A floating panel or a widget lets you copy a clip without leaving the app you are in.

4. Privacy

Your clipboard can hold passwords and codes. Check where clips are stored (on the device, or on someone's server), whether the app skips clips marked as passwords, and, if it uses Accessibility, what it says that permission is used for.

5. Sync and backup

If you also work on a PC, check whether the app syncs with a desktop version and how — through your own cloud storage or the developer's servers. A backup file you can restore on a new phone is worth having too.

Is the keyboard's clipboard enough?

Many keyboards have a clipboard of their own. Gboard, for example, shows recent clips above the keys but removes unpinned items after about an hour. For something you copied a few minutes ago it is the quickest option; for a history that stays, use a clipboard manager.

Questions

Can an Android app save everything I copy automatically?

Not by reading the clipboard in the background — Android 10 and later block that. Apps either save when you bring them to the front, or use an Accessibility service that notices the moment you copy. Clipboard+ offers the second as an optional Auto-save.

Is it safe to use a clipboard manager?

Choose one that keeps clips on your device or in your own storage, skips clips marked as passwords, and explains what any Accessibility permission is used for. Delete sensitive clips you no longer need.

Do I need a clipboard manager if my keyboard has a clipboard?

The keyboard's clipboard is fine for something you copied minutes ago. A clipboard manager keeps a history until you delete it, with search, pinning and images.
