Reading ruler for dyslexia and ADHD on Android: keep your place while reading

Updated 2026-10-14

Long text on a phone screen is easy to get lost in: your eyes slip to the next line, skip a row, or come back to a sentence you already read. Many people with dyslexia or ADHD use a ruler or a card under the line when reading on paper. On a phone, a reading ruler does the same job on screen.

Start with your display settings

Android's accessibility and display settings — larger font size, bold text, dark theme — can make text easier to read. They don't, however, mark the line you are on, which is what helps when your eyes keep wandering.

Add a reading ruler with ReadFocus+

ReadFocus+ (our app) floats a focus strip on top of any app — ebooks, PDFs, browsers, news, webtoons. Choose Line Highlight to light up the line you are reading, or Reading Mask to dim everything except your reading zone.

  1. Install ReadFocus+ from Google Play and allow "Display over other apps".
  2. Pick a mode: Line Highlight (a reading ruler) or Reading Mask.
  3. Open the app you're reading in and drag the strip to your line.
  4. Adjust its height (50–300 dp), opacity and color theme until it feels comfortable.

Free and Pro

The free version includes both focus modes and 3 color themes. Pro removes the ads and adds all 10 themes, strip height and opacity adjustment, and keep-screen-on.

Questions

Does a reading ruler help with dyslexia?

Many readers with dyslexia or ADHD find that marking the current line makes it easier to keep their place. It is a reading aid, not a treatment — try it and keep the settings that feel comfortable.

Which apps does ReadFocus+ work with?

Any app on screen: ebook and PDF readers, browsers, news, study notes, comics and webtoons.
