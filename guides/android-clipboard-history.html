How to see your clipboard history on Android

Updated 2026-10-21

Android itself remembers only the last thing you copied — copy something new and the previous text is gone. If you want to get back an address, a code or a sentence you copied earlier, you need a place that keeps a history. There are two: your keyboard, and a clipboard manager app.

Option 1: the clipboard in your keyboard

Many keyboards have their own clipboard. In Gboard, tap the clipboard icon in the toolbar above the keys (turn it on the first time). Samsung Keyboard has a clipboard button in its toolbar too.

This is the quickest way to grab something you copied a few minutes ago. The catch is that keyboard clipboards are short-term: Gboard, for example, removes unpinned items after about an hour, and you only see the list while you are typing.

Option 2: a clipboard manager that keeps a real history

If you copy the same things again and again — addresses, account numbers, canned replies — a clipboard manager keeps them until you delete them, lets you search them, and can show them over any app.

Clipboard+ (our app) keeps the text and images you copy in a history, newest first, with search and a floating panel you can open over the app you are using.

One catch: since Android 10, only the app on screen (and the keyboard you're typing with) can read the clipboard. Clipboard+ gets around waiting for you with an optional Auto-save: turn it on in Settings and, with a one-time Accessibility permission, everything you copy is saved the moment you copy it. Without Auto-save, bring the app to the front right after you copy.

  1. Install Clipboard+ from Google Play and open it once.
  2. Turn on Auto-save in Settings (optional) and copies are saved automatically. Otherwise, save right after you copy: tap the floating bubble at the edge of the screen, tap the save button on the home-screen widget, or open the app. For text, selecting it in another app and choosing Clipboard+ from the selection menu always works, because it skips the clipboard entirely.
  3. Open the floating panel (or the app) to see your history, tap a clip to copy it again, and paste it where you are.
  4. Keep things you reuse as snippets in groups, so they are always one tap away.

Which one should you use?

For a one-off — pasting something you copied a minute ago — the keyboard clipboard is enough. If you lose clips you wanted, or you keep retyping the same text, a clipboard manager saves that time. Clipboard+ is free to start; Pro adds sync with the Windows app through your own Google Drive, unlimited image clips and PDF export.

Questions

Does Android have a built-in clipboard history?

Not in the system itself — Android keeps only the latest clip. Keyboards such as Gboard and Samsung Keyboard add a short-term clipboard list.

Can I see clipboard history from my phone on my PC?

Yes, with Clipboard+ on both: the Android app and Clipboard+ for Windows sync through your own Google Drive (a Pro feature on Android).

I granted every permission, but not everything I copy gets saved.

That's the privacy rule Android introduced in version 10: an app that isn't on screen can't read the clipboard, and ordinary permissions don't change that. Turn on Auto-save in Clipboard+ Settings — it uses the Accessibility service to save each copy as you make it. Otherwise save right after copying with the bubble, the widget or by opening the app, or use Clipboard+ in the text selection menu. The system clipboard only holds the last item, so without Auto-save, copying something else first means the earlier item is gone.
